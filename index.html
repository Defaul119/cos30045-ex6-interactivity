Week 6 Lab — Filters and tooltips

Two charts of the same 4,233 TV models. The histogram (Exercise 6.1) gets filter buttons for screen technology (6.2); the scatterplot (6.3) gets a legend and a tooltip for each TV (6.4).

Exercises 6.1 and 6.2 · Histogram with filters

How much energy does a TV use in a year?

Most TVs use 200 to 600 kWh a year; the median is 400. A long tail reaches 1,800 kWh, and one 114-inch TV uses 2,652 kWh, alone in the last bin. Pick a screen technology to see its models only.

Bins are 200 kWh/year wide and stay the same for every filter. The y-axis rescales to fit the selected technology (there are 3,492 LED, 455 LCD and 286 OLED models), so read the axis before comparing bar heights between filters.

Exercises 6.3 and 6.4 · Scatterplot with tooltips

Do more stars mean less energy?

Yes: the median TV uses 1,135 kWh a year at 1 star, 422 kWh at 4 stars and 200 kWh at 7 stars. Each star rating is a column of TVs of different sizes. Hover over a circle to see the model.

Source: Ex6_TVdata_withStar.csv, made by the tutor's KNIME workflow (knime/W6 TV Data.knwf) from the Energy Rating register of televisions (tv_2026_02_15.csv), data.gov.au.
